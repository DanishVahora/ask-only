Every company promises to delete your data.
None of them can prove they did.

That is a theorem, not a complaint. Broadbent and Islam proved certified deletion is impossible using classical information, because classical ciphertexts can be copied. What the industry calls proof of deletion is a form with somebody’s phone number on it.

You can never prove you deleted it.
You can prove you never held it.

How it works

Three steps, and none of them move your document.

  1. 01

    Seal it

    The document encrypts in your browser with a passphrase that never leaves the page. What gets stored is ciphertext carrying its own expiry.

  2. 02

    Hand out a question

    Not a copy — a capability. One question set, a few uses, an expiry. Who may ask what is an on-chain role, so revoking is revoking a role.

  3. 03

    Let it die

    Past its death block the network stops returning it and every operation fails. There is nothing to delete, and nobody to trust to do it.

Keeping a permission alive leaves a mark.
Letting it die does not.

Arkiv emits a public event when a record is extended, and nothing at all when one expires. So retention is a signed, timestamped act — and silence means the permission was allowed to lapse. The burden of proof inverts.

What we don’t claim

The boundary, before you find it.

  • We do not erase bytes from history. Expiry means queries stop returning the record and operations fail. The document is ciphertext encrypted in your browser — that is what protects the bytes.
  • The enclave does not enforce our policy. 0G is an inference router, not a place to deploy code. Enforcement lives at the client and the verifier, and we say so.
  • 0G does not sign the answer. Its router exposes no signature endpoint — we probed eight paths with a live request id, all 404. So a receipt is bound by provider address, never by a signature we never got.
  • Expiry kills the capability, not knowledge. A landlord told “yes” still knows “yes”. We shrink what is disclosed to one bit. Nothing makes knowledge revocable.

Good questions.

Isn’t this just ZK selective disclosure?

ZK needs an issuer to sign structured data. India's real documents are unsigned, unstructured scans — payslips, bank statements, rent agreements. There is no circuit for a photograph of a payslip and no issuer signature to build one over. A model inside an enclave is the first universal adapter from real paper to one bit.

What stops you faking a receipt?

At the router level, nothing — and we say so on this page. What we do prove is narrower and real: the enclave address that answered, checked live against the attested provider list, and the verifier's own signature, which they produce before they are shown the answer. We cannot prove the enclave said it. We can prove the landlord asked and was told.

Does your server see my document?

Not when you run it yourself. Your browser decrypts locally and calls the enclave directly with your own key, so the server is not in the answer path at all. A hosted copy has to relay, which makes whoever runs it a trusted party — so the hosted build says exactly that in a permanent banner.

What happens when a permission expires?

The network stops returning it. Not a flag, not a tombstone — the query comes back empty and every operation on the record fails. We measured it: queryable at block 283073, gone at 283075.

Can I use this today?

It is a working prototype built for ETHGlobal Mumbai 2026, running on live testnets. The code is MIT. We are not a registered consent manager and cannot be — that requires ₹2 crore of net worth — so think of this as the reference implementation a registrant would run.
